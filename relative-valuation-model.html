Equity multiples apply directly to equity measures. Enterprise-value multiples convert to equity value by subtracting net debt, then divide by diluted shares. These methods are not averaged because they measure different things.

Match the measure to the business

MeasureWhat it comparesWhen it can helpMain caution
P/EShare price to earnings per shareCompanies with positive, reasonably stable earningsEPS is an input, not a valuation ratio by itself. P/E is not meaningful with negative EPS.
EV/EBITDAEnterprise value to EBITDAProfitable non-financial businesses where capital structures differNot meaningful with negative EBITDA; ignores capital spending and working-capital needs.
EV/Sales or P/SEnterprise or equity value to revenueCompanies with positive revenue but low or negative earningsRevenue alone says little about margins, growth quality, cash burn, or debt.
P/B or P/TBVShare price to book or tangible book value per shareBanks and some asset-heavy businessesInterpret with profitability, asset quality, and accounting marks. Tangible book definitions must match.
D/EInterest-bearing debt to book equityLeverage and balance-sheet risk contextIt is not a price multiple. Debt definitions vary, and bank capital analysis needs sector-specific measures.

For banks, deposits and borrowing are part of operations, so EV/EBITDA is usually a poor fit. Analysts often examine P/B, P/TBV, and P/E alongside return on equity, capital adequacy, asset quality, and net interest margin. For early-stage technology companies, EV/Sales or P/S may be usable when revenue is meaningful, but compare growth, gross margins, retention, and funding needs as well. A chosen multiple does not make unlike companies comparable.

Further reading: CFA Institute on price and enterprise-value multiples and Aswath Damodaran on financial-services companies.

Choosing comparable companies

A useful peer group has business models, growth, profitability, risk, and capital needs that are reasonably comparable. An industry label alone is not enough. Consider why each peer belongs in the group and remove companies whose accounting or business mix makes the comparison misleading.

Market data must be aligned by date and currency. Match each multiple's numerator and denominator: equity value to equity metrics for P/E, P/S, P/B, and P/TBV; enterprise value to EBITDA or sales for EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales. Inputs on this page are not live market data.

Read the range carefully

The low and high values reflect the lowest and highest positive peer multiples entered, while the center uses the median. A wide range can indicate that the selected peers or their circumstances differ. Multiples are affected by growth, risk, margins, and returns on capital. They summarize market pricing at a point in time; they do not establish what a company should be worth.

For a cash-flow approach, use the DCF valuation model. It uses forecast FCFF, WACC, and terminal growth rather than market peer multiples.
