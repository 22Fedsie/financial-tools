Use trailing twelve-month inputs for the target and peers. Multiples should use the same date, currency, accounting basis, and share-count convention. Example values are fictional and are not connected to market data.

Peer multiples

Use positive multiples only. Leave a multiple blank if it is not meaningful for that peer, such as P/E for a company with negative earnings. A method is unavailable when the target metric it needs is zero or negative.

P/E uses target diluted EPS. EV/EBITDA applies the multiple to target EBITDA, then subtracts net debt. P/S applies the multiple to target revenue. The model does not average the three methods because each has distinct limitations.

Choosing comparable companies

A useful peer group has business models, growth, profitability, risk, and capital needs that are reasonably comparable. An industry label alone is not enough. Consider why each peer belongs in the group and remove companies whose accounting or business mix makes the comparison misleading.

Market data must be aligned by date and currency. Match each multiple's numerator and denominator: equity value to equity metrics for P/E and P/S, and enterprise value to EBITDA for EV/EBITDA. Inputs on this page are not live market data.

Read the range carefully

The low and high values reflect the lowest and highest positive peer multiples entered, while the center uses the median. A wide range can indicate that the selected peers or their circumstances differ. Multiples summarize market pricing at a point in time; they do not establish what a company should be worth.

For a cash-flow approach, use the DCF valuation model. It uses forecast FCFF, WACC, and terminal growth rather than market peer multiples.

For education only. This model uses assumptions supplied by the visitor. It does not provide a company-specific recommendation or tell you to buy, hold, or sell a security.
