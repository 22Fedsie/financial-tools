Prepare your price file

The first column must be named Date and contain dates in YYYY-MM-DD format. Each other column is a ticker symbol, with one adjusted closing price per date. Each ticker must have a price for every date in the file. Include at least two dates and one ticker. Include every trading session in the period for the volatility estimate to be useful. You can use the sample data above to see the format.

Use adjusted close where available. Adjusted prices account for historical stock splits and distributions in the price series. If your data source provides only closing prices, dividends may be excluded from the results. This page does not download or verify market data.

How to interpret the measures

  • Total return compares beginning and ending portfolio value for fixed share quantities.
  • Annualized return converts the full-period compound change into a yearly rate based on elapsed calendar days.
  • Annualized volatility scales the standard deviation of daily portfolio returns by the square root of 252 trading days. It is a historical measure of variability, not a forecast.
  • Maximum drawdown is the largest observed decline from a previous portfolio value peak during the supplied period.
  • Contribution shows how each holding's return and starting portfolio weight combine to produce total portfolio return.

The analysis assumes the prices share the same currency, dates, and valuation basis. The fixed share quantities assume no rebalancing or transactions between the first and last dates. The model reports percentage returns and portfolio weights. It does not report account dollar values because adjusted historical prices may be restated for distributions and splits. Results exclude taxes and fees. This is an educational tool, not personalized investment advice.
