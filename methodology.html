How the estimates work

Methods, assumptions, and limits.

Each result comes from the values entered and simplified assumptions. Change an assumption and the result can change. Actual costs, tax treatment, returns, and lender terms may differ.

Mortgage payment

Uses the entered principal, annual rate, and amortization. The rate converts from Canadian semi-annual compounding to a monthly rate, then uses the standard fixed-payment formula. It assumes the rate and schedule stay unchanged and excludes property tax, insurance, mortgage default insurance, fees, and other ownership costs.

Investment growth and savings goal

Converts the effective annual return to an equivalent monthly rate. The balance grows monthly and contributions are added at month end. It estimates the contribution needed for the selected savings goal. Returns are hypothetical and exclude fees, taxes, inflation, and changes in returns.

Open the investment calculator →

Debt payoff

Divides the annual interest rate by 12, rounds monthly interest and payments to cents, and pays the balance down with a fixed monthly payment. The final payment is limited to the remaining amount. Lenders may calculate interest daily or apply payments differently; fees are excluded.

Open the debt calculator →

Rent versus buy

Projects home value from the entered annual change, subtracts mortgage balance and estimated selling costs, and compares owner equity with a renter's investment portfolio. The less expensive monthly housing scenario invests the difference. Rent increases yearly; owner costs stay fixed. Tax effects, repairs beyond the input, mortgage insurance, and other costs are excluded.

Open the rent-versus-buy calculator →

RRSP versus TFSA

Compares the same take-home budget invested monthly. TFSA contributions equal the budget. RRSP contributions are grossed up at the entered current marginal rate, assuming the full deduction is reinvested, then estimated withdrawal tax is applied. It assumes rates stay unchanged and excludes limits, fees, account rules, and individual tax details.

Open the RRSP-versus-TFSA calculator →

Net worth

Adds entered asset amounts and subtracts entered debts. Values are not verified; anything omitted is not counted.

Portfolio returns

Holding-period return is (ending value minus beginning value plus income received) divided by beginning value. Annualized return compounds that result over the entered date range. XIRR estimates the annualized money-weighted return using dated investor cash flows. Time-weighted return links period returns after removing end-period external flows. Its annualized result assumes equally spaced periods.

HPR assumes no external contributions or withdrawals during the period. For XIRR, enter contributions as negative investor cash flows and withdrawals or distributions as positive cash flows. For time-weighted return, contributions are positive portfolio flows and withdrawals are negative. The period-end flow assumption matters.

Multi-stock portfolio analyzer

Uses adjusted closing prices and fixed share quantities to estimate portfolio return over the supplied history. Each holding's return is its final adjusted price divided by its first adjusted price, minus one. Starting portfolio weights are based on each holding's starting adjusted price and share quantity. Contributions equal each holding's return multiplied by its starting weight. Annualized return compounds the total portfolio return over elapsed calendar days. Annualized volatility is the sample standard deviation of successive daily portfolio returns multiplied by the square root of 252. Maximum drawdown compares each portfolio value with the previous running peak.

The analyzer assumes all prices use one currency, include every trading session, and share the same date range. Fixed quantities mean the portfolio is not rebalanced. Adjusted prices may be restated, so the model reports returns and weights rather than account dollar values. Data is processed in the browser.

CAPM and WACC

CAPM cost of equity equals the risk-free rate plus beta multiplied by the expected market risk premium. WACC weights the cost of equity and the after-tax cost of debt using the entered equity weight and the remaining debt weight. The model does not estimate beta, market risk premium, financing mix, or debt cost.

Open the CAPM and WACC calculator →

Discounted cash flow

Forecast FCFF equals after-tax operating income, plus depreciation and amortization, less capital spending and working capital investment. Working capital investment is approximated as a percentage of the annual revenue change. Forecast FCFF is discounted by WACC. Terminal value uses the Gordon growth formula and is unavailable when growth is greater than or equal to WACC. Net debt is subtracted from enterprise value before dividing by diluted shares.

This simplified FCFF model may not suit banks and insurers, where debt and regulatory capital are part of operations. It omits several company-specific claims and does not estimate WACC.

Relative valuation

Uses entered positive peer multiples to show low, median, and high implied share values separately for P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/S, P/B, and P/TBV. P/E multiplies target diluted EPS. Enterprise-value multiples apply to EBITDA or revenue, subtract net debt, and divide by diluted shares. Equity multiples apply to revenue, book equity, or tangible book equity and divide by diluted shares. Debt-to-equity is shown only as leverage context and does not produce an implied share value. A method is unavailable when its target metric is missing or non-positive. The selected company profile gives guidance but does not automatically validate a peer group. The model does not select peers or provide live data.

Open the relative valuation model →

Black-Scholes

Estimates theoretical European call or put values using the underlying and strike prices, time to expiration, risk-free rate, volatility, and continuous dividend yield. It assumes constant volatility and rates and does not model early exercise, discrete dividends, trading costs, or changing market conditions.
